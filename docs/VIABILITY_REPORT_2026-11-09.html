Market: size, structure, health

The care market is large and growing. The software slice is not. Confusing the two is the single most common error in this category's pitch decks.

3.1 Buyer base — strong, growing, fragmenting downwards

The segments MeticleCare targets are the growing ones — homecare +42% since 2019, supported living +20% in the last year. But they are fragmenting downwards: more micro-providers. A long tail of tiny buyers means low ACV, high support cost and high involuntary churn.

3.2 The software slice — a bottom-up TAM, and a funnel that ends small

Neither DHSC nor CQC publishes a care-software spend series. Syndicated "market reports" quoting USD 0.9–1.2bn for "social work management tools" or "private duty care software" use US/local-authority case-management definitions and should not be used as a TAM. A defensible bottom-up estimate:

New in this report — the four-nations TAM, now sized 9 Nov

The 5 Nov research flagged that the four-nations differentiator "rests on a TAM that has not been sized here". Using a 2025 UK-wide homecare service count cross-checked against a Welsh parliamentary answer: ~18,400 homecare services across the UK — 16,001 England, 1,289 Scotland, 776 Wales, 331 Northern Ireland. The devolved nations therefore add only ~2,400 services, ~13% to the English base. (Wales: 655 of those domiciliary services are limited companies, per Senedd WQ97755, 1 Dec 2025; NI: ~42,000 practitioners across ~500 employers, NISCC Jun 2025.)

Conclusion: the four-nations uplift to software TAM is roughly +10–15% (~£8–18m/yr), taking UK-wide TAM to ~£90–140m/yr — still not venture scale. Worse, those buyers sit behind three separate regulators, three procurement regimes, no shared assured list, and Welsh-language "Active Offer" obligations, so the addressable uplift is smaller and the cost-to-serve higher. Four-nations is a real engineering achievement but a small commercial one. It should be sold as credibility, not as market expansion. Caveat: the England figure here (16,001 services) differs from CQC's official 13,500+ (Apr 2026) — different service/location definitions. Devolved figures are secondary/vendor-sourced; treat the ratio as indicative.

3.3 Buyers are in financial distress — the underweighted risk

  • Council homecare rate £25.05/hr for 2026/27 vs the Homecare Association's £34.42/hr Minimum Price — a £9.37/hr gap on the 70–80% of hours bought by councils/NHS. Only 1% of reviewed council contracts met the minimum in 2025/26.
  • £715m adult social care budget overspend in 2025/26; 66% of councils saw providers close, cease trading or hand back contracts in the six months to May 2026; >400,000 people waiting for assessment/care.
  • 33 homecare company insolvencies (SIC 881) in Jan–Aug 2026 — more than any full year since 2016; 63 care-company insolvencies including residential vs 36 in the same period of 2025. Secondary analysis of Insolvency Service data; directionally credible.
  • National Living Wage £12.71/hr from 1 Apr 2026, employer NIC at 15%, homecare vacancy rate still ~9%.

Implication: a SaaS book in this sector suffers involuntary churn from customer insolvency, and price rises/upsell face structural resistance. Any model assuming smooth net revenue retention is optimistic. Care homes are a healthier two-tier exception (27% profitability, self-funder share 48%→52%) — but that is not MeticleCare's primary ICP.

3.4 Policy tailwind, but no near-term budget event

2025 Spending Review: >£4bn extra ASC funding by 2028/29 and £500m for the first Fair Pay Agreement (effective April 2028) — real, but back-loaded. The Casey Commission launched its "Big Conversation on Care" on 29 July 2026, with a final report now expected summer 2027; the funding architecture remains unresolved after decades of commissions. The Health and Care Worker visa route closed to new overseas entrants on 22 July 2025, and international recruitment fell 105,000 (2023/24) → 50,000 (2024/25) → ~30,000 (2025/26): good for "admin efficiency" narratives, bad for provider cost bases. No new near-term digitisation funding event was found in the 9 Nov check.

Demand: real, but the land-grab is over

This is the most important section. The adoption curve is the verdict.

The 20% who have not bought are disproportionately micro-providers — the exact group with the least ability to pay. The residual greenfield is a price/access gap, not a product gap.

4.2 The "mandate" is weaker than the pitch deck assumes

Verified primary sources, 9 November 2026

DHSC's own buyer guidance (published 29 Sep 2026) states plainly: "There is no formal public sector process for DSCR procurements"; "You may be able to negotiate the price with the supplier"; and for in-house systems, "there is currently no requirement for your solution to be on the assured solutions list." It also confirms "DSCRs do not normally include rostering or electronic medication administration records (eMAR)" — which is the real, documented gap MeticleCare's bundle addresses.

  • The original "80% digitised by March 2024" was a policy aim, not a statutory requirement (DHSC, Jun 2022); it was missed and reached ~16 months late.
  • The Digitising Social Care (DiSC) programme ended in April 2025; the current formulation is an "ambition for all care providers to be fully digitised by the end of this Parliament" (ministerial answer, 15 Dec 2025).
  • CQC requires accurate, complete records under Regulation 17 — paper is compliant; CQC does not endorse any system.
  • The genuinely binding pressure is contractual: DSPT "Standards Met" by 30 June is written into many LA/ICB contracts, and GP Connect access requires an assured DSCR + DSPT "standards met" + signed National Data Sharing Agreement (verified verbatim in the 29 Sep 2026 DHSC guidance).
  • 2025/26 public money funds training, not licences: a Level 5 Digital Leadership qualification, £12m LDSS training and £12m workforce courses (DHSC, 9 Apr 2025). The old ICB match-funded grant rounds no longer run in that form.

Conclusion: the government-subsidised adoption wave that created the incumbents' installed bases cannot be ridden again. This is the single most important demand finding, and it is the reason a new horizontal DSCR entrant is fighting for replacement rather than expansion.

05 — Competition

Competition and the credibility gate

On 9 November 2026 I pulled the live NHS England assured-solutions list and counted it. This changed the detail, not the direction — it sharpened it.

5.1 The gate: 23 assured products, verified live

The assured list currently carries 23 DSCR products (December 2025: 21). Access appears twice. Every listed product offers core care planning, recording, reporting and audit trails, meets national quality/security standards, and can connect to GP records. The list is a moving target: NHS England "regularly reviews these standards" and may remove products.

Verified 9 Nov 2026: Access Care and Clinical · Access Care Planning · Birdie · Care Control Systems · Care Vision · Careberry · CareDocs · CareLineLive · Cura Systems · Dom Portal by Decaura · Fusion eCare Solutions · Leecare Platinum6 · Log my Care · Nourish · OneTouch · PASS by everyLIFE · Person Centred Software · QCS (formerly Carebeans) · Qwikify · RoundSys · sorted.care · SOS Care Management System · Tagtronics (TagCare)

Two findings that matter more than the count:

  • The exact bundle already exists, assured. sorted.care describes "care records, medication, rostering, HR, training, recruitment, surveys, policies, quality assurance and reporting plus a carer app" — essentially MeticleCare's module list. Birdie covers care management, quality auditing, rostering, finance and analytics; Dom Portal by Decaura explicitly covers "domiciliary care, live-in care, and supported living care".
  • The installed base is enormous. PASS claims 84,000 carers nationwide; Person Centred Software claims 8,000+ UK providers supporting 300,000 people; Birdie claims 1,000+ providers. Vendor-published marketing figures, but they indicate the order of magnitude of the displacement task.
Every gap MeticleCare positions on is occupied

Supported-living workflows, CQC evidence automation, family portal, chat, training matrix, multi-service providers, four-nations compliance. These are feature gaps, not market gaps, and the largest incumbents can copy them within a sprint. Two 2026 entrants — Flocare and CareGovern — already target supported living specifically; neither appears on the assured list, which is a small opening but also means they face the same gate.

MeticleCare's planned tiers (£99 / £299 / £749 per month) sit inside the observed range, but four structural problems:

  • £99 is not disruptive — it is £99 more than free, and only £21 below CareLineLive's floor, in a market where 70% cite licence cost as a barrier and 82% want ongoing subsidies.
  • £749 "Enterprise" is too cheap: 50 carers on CareLineLive BYOD already cost ~£985/month plus onboarding. A flat cap under-monetises large multi-site groups and signals "SMB product" to enterprise buyers.
  • Flat tiers ignore the market's revenue-linked units (care hours / carers / service users / beds). DHSC's own guidance confirms charges are normally based on staff, system users, clients or bed capacity — inviting adverse selection and leaving money on the table at the top.
  • £99/month cannot credibly fund assurance, customer DSPT support, ISO 27001, Cyber Essentials Plus, GP Connect, 24/7 UK support, data migration and retraining. Price is not the barrier — credibility is.

5.4 The credibility checklist — a 12–24 month hurdle pricing cannot shortcut

To be credible to a UK care provider in 2026 a vendor needs: assured-list inclusion; DSPT ("Standards Met", own plus customer support); live GP Connect integration; PRSB conformance; ISO 27001; Cyber Essentials Plus; UK data hosting; offline mode; tamper-proof audit trails; 24/7 UK support; funded migration and retraining; an interoperability roadmap. Incumbents actively neutralise the challenger pitch: PASS gives free migration, ~30-day go-live and 3 months free; CareLineLive offers free migration; CareDocs runs champion-led training.

Against that checklist, your own documents record the gap: the Compliance Badges page correctly states "we have not submitted the DSPT"; the legal/commercial set is unstarted "before the first paying customer"; add-ons cannot be sold because no Stripe mechanism exists; MARKET_READINESS_AUDIT.md (13 Oct 2026) rates legal/commercial blocked, Play data safety blocked, iOS listing not started, backups real but no restore ever run.

06 — The math

Investment math and exit path

Scored /5 on the dimensions a 2026 seed/Series-A investor would actually underwrite.

The internal model's £542k-to-break-even at month 30 assumes a sales motion this research does not support: entering a market where 80% have bought, 23 assured competitors exist, 12-month contracts are the norm, incumbents give migration away free, and the buyer base is insolvent at a record rate. A £3–5m ARR business could be a credible £30–50m strategic acquisition at ~9.5× ARR — but only with a defensible data/workflow asset and real contracts, not a lighter DSCR.

What investors need to see before a Series A in 2026

£1m+ ARR · >100% growth · NRR >120% · CAC payback <12–18 months · multi-year council/group contracts. MeticleCare is currently at zero on all five. The realistic path is SEIS/EIS seed → specialist healthtech/SaaS fund → strategic acquisition, not an independent scale story. The acquirer set is genuinely active (Hg, Access, Cow Corner/PCS, Brighton Park Capital, Livingbridge) — that is the strongest part of the investment case.

07 — The other side

What is genuinely strong

This is not a "kill it" report. Five things are real assets.

1 · Engineering depth is a real asset

Very few pre-revenue products ship RLS multi-tenancy, MFA, offline mobile sync with idempotency, an audit-log-before-response guarantee, 1,500+ tests, a claims guard that polices its own marketing, and a live production deployment. This is a credible team + codebase asset with acqui-hire value.

2 · The bundle is coherent

Rota → care record → medication → compliance evidence → billing is genuinely one data model. DHSC explicitly acknowledges most DSCRs do not include rostering or eMAR; point-solution reconciliation is a real, documented cost.

3 · Compliance/privacy posture is unusually thoughtful

Four-nations frameworks, on-demand location privacy, DPIAs, a governed AI capability registry with method disclosure. In a regulated, trust-based sale that matters — even if it is not a purchase criterion on its own.

4 · The market need is not imaginary

An ageing population, 400,000+ people waiting for assessment/care, a large uninspected CQC backlog and a structural workforce shortage all keep the category alive.

5 · The acquirer set is active

Hg (Nourish), Access, Cow Corner/PCS and Brighton Park Capital are buying. A niche asset with real ARR has an exit.

08 — The decision

The 90-day decision gate

Cheap, non-engineering, decisive. Do not commit the full £400–650k raise or continue broad feature completion on the current thesis. More engineering will not change the binding constraints, which are, in order: customer proof, regulatory credibility, and a defensible wedge.

Kill criteria

If after 90 days there is no paying pilot, no LOI, or buyers consistently say "we already have a system and would not migrate", stop the horizontal all-in-one and move to one of: (a) pivot to a narrow compliance/outcomes/interoperability layer that sits on top of assured incumbents; (b) sell the codebase/team as an acqui-hire to an assured-list incumbent; (c) keep it as a low-cost, self-funded niche product with no outside capital.

If you proceed anyway, only two positions are defensible

  1. Narrow wedge, not replacement. A supported-living outcomes / tenancy / four-nations evidence layer that integrates with Birdie/PASS/Nourish rather than displacing them. Higher credibility, lower switching cost — but Flocare and CareGovern already occupy this niche, and supported living was only 6% of the DHSC survey base.
  2. Multi-service / mixed-provider consolidation play. Sell to consolidating mid-tier groups and multi-site community providers (who have a budget owner and a group-level pain), not to the 30% of homecare services supporting ≤4 people. Pair with international expansion from day one, because the UK-only TAM is too small for venture scale.

What not to do: do not underwrite the plan on the £13.4bn care-delivery market or USD-billion "care software" reports; do not lead with price; do not lead with "AI"; do not spend the raise before the 90-day evidence exists.

10 — Honesty

Risks, caveats, and what would change the verdict

Caveats on this research

  • No traction evidence was found. Absence of LOIs/pilots/customers in the reviewed documents is not proof there are none. If real customers exist, the verdict changes materially and the gate can be shortened.
  • TAM/SOM are analyst estimates with stated assumptions. No official UK care-software market size exists; £80–120m and £0.8–3.5m capturable ARR are illustrative, not sourced facts.
  • Some statistics are secondary analyses of official data (Insolvency Service via Care Brief; ADASS/Care England via trade press). Directionally credible, not primary-verified here.
  • Vendor pricing, customer counts and four-nations service counts are vendor/secondary-published. The 16,001 England homecare figure conflicts with CQC's official 13,500+ on definitions; use the ratio, not the absolute.
  • England-only bias in most official series; the devolved-nation sizing added here is indicative only.
  • Ireland commencement status unverified in the 9 Nov external check; internal research is dated 28 Sep 2026 and explicitly warns the date has slipped before.
  • Internal financial figures (£400–650k, £542k, month 30, £99/£299/£749) come from repository docs dated Aug–Oct 2026 and were not independently re-verified.

What would flip this to a GO

  • One or more paying customers at a real price, with a documented switching trigger and a named budget owner.
  • A signed design-partner LOI from a multi-site or consolidating group (a buyer with a real budget).
  • DSPT submitted and a dated, costed assured-solution plan — evidence the 12–24 month gate is being attacked.
  • A narrow ICP with a unit-based price and a CAC payback that survives the sector's insolvency churn.
  • A dated Irish commencement confirmed by a solicitor, turning the one greenfield window into a real timetable.
  • Re-score ≥3.5/5 on real pipeline numbers rather than desk estimates.

Any two of these, evidenced, would justify revisiting the raise. One of them alone would not.
