# International growth plan — INNF 8.3(d)

**Status:** internal plan — evidence for the INNF 8.3(d) endorsement file.
Prepared 28 September 2026. Sequenced directly from
`MULTI_JURISDICTION_EXPANSION_RESEARCH.md` (evidence verified 28 Sep 2026). The
expansion rule that governs this plan, restated from that research: **no
jurisdiction without its vetting seed.**

The endorsement asks for "growth into national and international markets." This
document shows the planning structure: the national plan is the four-nations
architecture already built; the international plan is a three-candidate
sequence, each gated on verifiable entry conditions rather than dates alone —
because the research's own finding is that dates slip.

## 1. National (UK four nations) — built, commercialising now

- England (CQC), Wales (CIW), Scotland (Care Inspectorate), Northern Ireland
  (RQIA): per-nation frameworks, per-nation vetting schemes
  (`compliance.vetting.ts`), and a per-regulator registrations table
  (migration 129).
- Commercial sequencing: England first (15,232 CQC-registered domiciliary
  organisations — see `INNF_8.3d_MARKET_RESEARCH.md`); Wales, Scotland and
  Northern Ireland on inbound demand.
- Gates that must clear before those pushes: T2-12 (PVG / AccessNI tier
  confirmation), T2-15 (CIW lines-of-enquiry wording), T2-16 (RQIA domain
  content).

## 2. International candidates, in order

### Candidate 1 — Ireland (HIQA): first

The window is open now: the Health Bill 2025 pathway brings home support into
HIQA registration for the first time. Every Irish homecare provider is about to
face, for the first time, the inspection-readiness burden our product automates.

Entry conditions — all required before committing a quarter:

1. **Commencement verified.** The Bill's timetable has slipped before; confirm
   current status (a 30-minute check) before anything else.
2. **Garda vetting seed built** — the cheapest vetting model of any candidate:
   one scheme, one issuer, one seed row.
3. **EU data residency proven** — the real cost: 3–5 weeks (tenancy-region
   routing plus an EU-region Postgres deployment). The AI processor allowlist is
   already per-deployment, which is the hard part done.
4. **One Irish design partner signed** via the pilot/LOI kit's outreach.

Jobs created: 2 sales, 1 customer success, 1 AI/ML, 1 ops/admin
(`INNF_8.3d_JOB_CREATION_PLAN.md`, Year 3).

### Candidate 2 — Australia (ACQSC): second

Aged Care Act 2024 (in force 1 Nov 2025) creates the same
"providers suddenly need inspection software" window, two years more mature.
The tiered registration system does not exist in our model — the readiness
scorer assumes one framework per organisation — so:

Entry conditions:

1. **One-week tier-modelling spike completed** and its cost estimate accepted.
   Do not commit before the spike; do not start the spike before Ireland's
   go/no-go, so engineering capacity is not split.
2. **A committed Australian lead** from outreach.
3. **Funding headroom after Ireland** (the model's cash trough is at Month 30).

Jobs created: 1 sales, 1 engineering (Year 3 and beyond).

### Candidate 3 — New Zealand: opportunistic

Cheapest lift, smallest market (~4.7m). Ngā paerewa standards mapping plus an
NZ Police vetting seed — again a single-scheme seed row. Entry condition: an
inbound lead, not a dedicated quarter. No jobs dedicated; absorbed by existing
roles.

## 4. What this plan deliberately does not do

- **It does not commit to dates.** Entry conditions are verifications and
  commercial events; the research's central lesson is that regulator timetables
  slip, and a plan keyed to dates hides that.
- **It does not enter a market without its vetting seed** — the standing
  expansion rule.
- **It does not split engineering capacity** across two international entries at
  once; Ireland completes before Australia begins.
