# INNF 8.2 — customer interview protocol

**Status:** working protocol — owner **Adetoye**. Prepared 28 September 2026.
**What this is for:** 8.2 requires evidence of "market awareness". The only honest
evidence of market awareness is a record of having talked to buyers. This protocol
turns conversations into a dated, citable evidence base. It is written so that a
*pilot conversion* is a natural by-product rather than the point.

**The rule:** 15–20 interviews with no sale is a pass on this limb. Zero
interviews with a great deck is a fail.

## 1. Who to talk to (15–20, over four weeks)

| Segment | n | Why this segment |
|---|---|---|
| Single-site domiciliary agency, 1–3 locations | 6 | Starter tier fit; the largest population of providers |
| Small group, 4–15 locations | 5 | Professional tier fit; the target buyer |
| Multi-branch, 15+ locations | 3 | Enterprise tier; the four-nations story |
| Supported living provider | 3 | Adjacent segment named in the pricing model |
| Wales / Scotland / Northern Ireland | 3 | Validates the four-nations claim — one per nation, no exceptions |

**Who does not count:** a provider who cannot buy within 12 months (wrong size,
wrong sector, no budget owner). An interview with someone who cannot buy is
research about a different market.

## 2. The questions (the same twelve, every interview)

1. Walk me through your last week of care visits — what happens, in order?
2. What does your team use today for rostering, visit records and notes? (list,
   not yes/no)
3. When a visit does not go to plan, who finds out, and how quickly?
4. Show me how you would prove to an inspector that a specific visit happened.
   (do not offer help; let them struggle)
5. If an inspector asked tomorrow for one worker's training record, how long to
   produce it?
6. What happens when a care worker's route changes mid-shift? (tests the core
   scheduling thesis)
7. Where do your workers complain about the software, or refuse to use it?
8. What is the single most expensive problem in your week that software could
   remove?
9. When your inspection visit comes, what is the worst-prepared part?
10. If a vendor told your workers "we don't track your location all day", how
    would they react — and how would *you* answer "then how do you know they
    arrived?" (location-privacy thesis, and the replacement question behind it)
11. What would a competitor have to do for you to switch systems within a year?
12. If we ran a free eight-week pilot on one rota, what would make you say yes —
    and what would make you say no?

**Rules:** questions before pitching (12 is the pitch); do not lead; record the
answer before the follow-up; silence is a valid answer — write "no comment".

## 3. Capturing it so it counts as evidence

- **Written notes within 24 hours**, same template every time (§4).
- **Verbatim quotes only where given** — ask "can I quote that?" and record the
  answer. No permission, no quotation marks.
- **No personal or service-user data. Ever.** Interview a provider's *process*,
  not their residents. If an individual story is volunteered, record it
  de-identified or leave it out.
- **Record what contradicted us.** The last field of every record is "what
  changed my mind". A file with no surprises is a sales conversation, not
  research, and an assessor reading it will see the difference.
- **Name, role and date recorded** (with permission), so any claim in the
  endorsement file is checkable by a third party.

## 4. One-page interview record

```
Provider: __________  Nation: ____  Locations: ____  Staff: ____
Contact + role: __________  Date: __________  Interviewer: __________
Q1–Q12 answers (notes; verbatim in quotes only with permission)
Misconceptions we had going in:
What we would change in the product:
Would they run a pilot? What would make them say no:
Consent to quote / to be named:
Follow-up + date:
```

## 5. Synthesis (after five, then after fifteen)

- **After five:** if a question is not producing answers, rewrite it. Stop asking
  what you want to hear.
- **After fifteen:** a findings note covering (a) what we believed, (b) what we
  learned, (c) what we changed, (d) how many would pilot and on what terms,
  (e) the pricing signal. **Include the pricing signal** — willingness to pay is
  the viability question and the only one here that has to be asked directly:
  *"what would this cost you per month, and what would that have to save you?"*
- Re-baseline the financial model against (e) where it disagrees with the
  £99 / £299 / £749 tiers. A model that survives contact with fifteen buyers is
  the point; one that does not is exactly what an assessor will find.

## 6. From interview to design partner

At interview six, offer the eight-week pilot and the LOI template
(`INNF_8.3d_PILOT_AND_LOI_KIT.md` §2 and §1). One signed design partner
converts more 8.2 evidence than any document we can write: market awareness,
demand, and a committed counterparty for the funding record in a single act.

**Then update the tracker** — T3-2 (LOIs) and the interview count in
`INNF_8.2_VIABILITY_EVIDENCE.md`. The two must not drift apart.
