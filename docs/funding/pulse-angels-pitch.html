What problem does MeticleCare solve?

That's two hours of her day gone before care begins."

The Concrete Problem

UK supported-living and domiciliary care providers manage their operations with fragmented, disconnected tools. A typical provider uses:

  • Paper MAR charts for medication administration records
  • Spreadsheets for rotas, scheduling, and shift management
  • Physical folders for compliance evidence, training records, and policies
  • Phone calls and WhatsApp for team communication and shift swaps
  • Paper forms for incidents, daily notes, and care records

This creates three measurable operational failures:

1. Admin consumes care capacity

Care managers spend approximately 40% of their working day on administration — filling forms, cross-referencing spreadsheets, chasing training records, preparing compliance evidence. That's 3+ hours per day not spent on care delivery, staff support, or service improvement. For a 24-bed provider paying a manager £35,000/year, that's £14,000/year spent on admin.

2. Compliance evidence is gathered reactively

When a CQC inspector arrives, providers scramble to assemble evidence from paper records, spreadsheets, and filing cabinets. The CQC Single Assessment Framework now requires continuous evidence from operational records — but providers have no way to generate this automatically. Missing evidence leads to lower ratings, enforcement actions, and in extreme cases, service closure.

3. Medication errors go undetected

Paper MAR charts make it difficult to track missed doses, late administrations, or PRN (as-needed) medication patterns in real time. A missed dose might not be discovered until the next shift handover — or worse, until a family member asks questions. The care sector reports medication errors as one of the most common incident types.

Question 2

Why MeticleCare?

Why can't a care provider simply use existing systems?

Existing Solutions Fall Into Three Categories

Category 1: Point Solutions

Person Centred Software does medication well but doesn't handle rotas, compliance evidence, or team communication. Plymouth software does care plans but not eMAR. Providers end up paying for 3–5 separate systems that don't talk to each other — and staff must learn multiple interfaces.

Category 2: Legacy Platforms

Access Group and OneAdvanced offer broad feature sets but built on legacy architecture. Complex pricing (per module + per user), slow innovation, poor mobile experience. Staff resist using them because the UX feels like 2010 software.

Category 3: Paper & Spreadsheets

The majority of small-to-medium providers still use paper and spreadsheets. Familiar, zero learning curve, but no audit trail, no compliance automation, and the admin burden compounds as the organisation grows.

What's Missing

No existing platform combines all operational workflows (rota + medication + compliance + notes + incidents + training + communication) in one connected system with predictable pricing and modern UX.
